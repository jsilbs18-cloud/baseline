# Federal Funding & Authorities Map

The catalog of tools the government can bring to a deal, organized by **instrument
type** (what it does economically) rather than by agency — because package design
starts from the deal's binding constraint, then finds the instrument that breaks it.

> **Maintenance note:** program capacity, appropriations, and rules change
> constantly. This map is a starting inventory from public information; the
> Incentives & Instruments team owns keeping a verified, current version (with
> program office POCs and real capacity numbers) in the CUI environment. Verify
> status with the owning agency and counsel before any package relies on a tool.

## How to read a deal's binding constraint → instrument family

| Binding constraint | Instrument family |
|---|---|
| Cost of capital / financeability | Credit programs (loans, guarantees) |
| Upfront capex hurdle | Grants, cost-sharing, tax credits |
| Revenue/demand uncertainty | Demand-side: offtakes, price floors, procurement, stockpiles |
| Timeline risk (permits) | Permitting acceleration |
| Strategic control / alignment | Equity and special positions |
| Workforce, sites, power | State partnership + targeted federal programs |

## 1. Credit programs (loans & guarantees)

| Tool | Agency | Best fit | Watchouts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title 17 loans/guarantees (LPO) | DOE | Energy, grid, nuclear, critical-materials processing with an energy nexus | Long diligence timeline; credit subsidy cost; start early |
| Advanced Technology Vehicles Mfg (ATVM) | DOE | Vehicle & component manufacturing | Statutory eligibility bounds |
| Office of Strategic Capital loans | DoD | Defense-critical component manufacturing, dual-use tech | Newer program; capacity and criteria evolving |
| Make More in America (domestic financing) | EXIM | Export-linked domestic manufacturing | Export nexus requirement; content rules |
| China & Transformational Exports Program | EXIM | Competing head-to-head with PRC-backed financing | Sector eligibility list |
| DFC | DFC | Critical-mineral and supply-chain projects abroad that feed US industry | Primarily overseas authority; useful for upstream links in a domestic deal's chain |
| TIFIA / RRIF | DOT | Enabling transport infrastructure around a mega-site | Infrastructure only, not the plant itself |
| USDA rural development & biorefinery programs | USDA | Rural-sited projects, bio-based manufacturing | Rural eligibility mapping |

## 2. Grants & cost-sharing

| Tool | Agency | Best fit | Watchouts |
|---|---|---|---|
| CHIPS incentives | Commerce (in-house) | Semiconductor fabrication, packaging, materials | Milestone-based; interacts with any equity/renegotiation posture |
| DPA Title III | DoD | Industrial-base capacity: critical materials, components, munitions supply chains | Presidential determination pathway; strong for speed |
| IBAS | DoD | Targeted industrial-base gaps | Smaller checks; good gap-filler in a stack |
| MESC programs | DOE | Battery, grid, energy supply-chain manufacturing | Appropriations-dependent; round timing |
| BARDA / HHS | HHS | Pharma ingredient and biomanufacturing onshoring | Health-security framing required |
| EDA (incl. Tech Hubs) | Commerce | Ecosystem/workforce/infrastructure around a deal; sub-scale referrals | Not sized for the deal itself; excellent complement |
| NSF Regional Innovation Engines | NSF | R&D ecosystem adjacent to a deal | Indirect; long horizon |

## 3. Tax instruments (with Treasury/IRS)

| Tool | Best fit | Watchouts |
|---|---|---|
| 48D (CHIPS ITC) | Semiconductor manufacturing capex | Statutory scope |
| 45X (advanced manufacturing production) | Battery, solar, inverter, critical-mineral production | Per-unit economics; verify current schedule |
| 48C (qualifying advanced energy project) | Clean-energy & industrial decarb capex | Allocation rounds; capped |
| 45Q and energy credits | Carbon capture, energy projects | Deal-specific |
| Opportunity Zone benefits | Location-dependent equity | Investor-side benefit, not project cash |

Tax tools are entitlement-like where the deal qualifies — often the cheapest federal
dollar in a package. Always compute them first; they shrink the discretionary ask.

## 4. Demand-side instruments (often the most powerful, least used)

| Tool | Agency | Best fit | Watchouts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offtake agreements / price floors | DoD and others | Commodity-exposed production (critical minerals) where price uncertainty blocks financing | Novel structures need careful authority and appropriations analysis |
| National Defense Stockpile purchases | DoD/DLA | Strategic materials | Stockpile funding cycles |
| Advance market commitments / procurement | Various | Anything the government ultimately buys (pharma, satellites, fuels, munitions) | Requirements process alignment |
| DPA Title I priority ratings | Commerce (delegated) | Assuring a deal's own supply chain delivers | Use sparingly; market distortion |

A credible price floor or committed customer can replace billions in subsidy —
demand certainty is what actually unlocks project finance for commodity-exposed
projects. This family deserves a dedicated specialist.

## 5. Equity & special positions

| Tool | Basis | Watchouts |
|---|---|---|
| Equity stakes linked to awards | Negotiated (e.g., renegotiated grant terms) | Valuation, governance rights, exit policy, appearance — Secretary-level decisions, always |
| Golden-share-type rights | Negotiated in transaction approvals | Scope rights narrowly and in writing; precedent-setting |
| Warrants / upside instruments | Negotiated alongside credit support | Aligns taxpayer upside; keep terms standard where possible |
| Milestone clawbacks | Standard award terms | The portfolio team's enforcement backbone |

This family is where the Accelerator is genuinely novel — and where precedent
discipline matters most. Every position taken becomes the template (and the
political argument) for the next one. The precedent library in `07-systems/` is
partly for exactly this.

## 6. Permitting & regulatory acceleration

| Tool | Owner | Use |
|---|---|---|
| FAST-41 covered-project designation | Permitting Council | Coordinated schedule + transparency dashboard for qualifying infrastructure |
| NEPA strategy (categorical exclusions, EA vs EIS scoping, lead-agency choice) | Per project | The permitting lead's core craft |
| Corps / EPA / state pre-application alignment | Per project | Front-load; late surprises are schedule killers |
| Defense-siting and other special pathways | Case-by-case | Where a defense nexus exists |

## 7. Non-federal layers

- **State & local incentives** — often the largest single layer after tax credits;
  coordinate through SelectUSA relationships and the state channel. Never let a
  company arbitrage states *and* the federal government separately — package talks
  should be three-way by the Structure stage.
- **Allied-government co-investment** — sovereign pledges (see origination) can
  co-anchor deals; adds diplomacy to the deal team's job.
- **Utility partnerships** — power availability is the binding constraint for AI
  data centers and advanced manufacturing alike; utility commitments belong in the
  package table like any instrument.

## Stacking guidance

1. **Sequence: tax → credit → demand-side → grant → equity.** Cheapest, most
   rule-based dollars first; discretionary and novel dollars only for the remaining
   gap. This maximizes leverage and minimizes appearance risk.
2. **Check stacking rules per pair** — some programs restrict combination or count
   other federal support against limits. The Incentives team maintains the
   compatibility matrix.
3. **One federal face.** However many agencies are in the stack, the company hears
   one coordinated position — that's the Accelerator's core value proposition.
4. **Every dollar has a milestone.** No instrument goes into a package without a
   condition the portfolio team can enforce later.
