# Strategic Importance Scoring & Tiering

The framework that turns "everything is important" into a defensible ranking. Its
job is not to compute truth — it's to force the same questions to be asked of every
deal, make disagreements explicit (argue about a score, not a vibe), and give the
Secretary a portfolio view that isn't just recency-ordered.

## Dimensions & weights (0–100 total)

| # | Dimension | Wt | What it measures | Anchor questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | National security criticality | 25 | Does this capability matter to defense, deterrence, or crisis resilience? | Is the output on a defense-critical list? Would loss of access degrade military capability? |
| 2 | Supply-chain dependency | 20 | Does it break a foreign chokepoint — especially adversary-controlled? | Current import share? Single-source? Adversary leverage? Post-deal domestic share? |
| 3 | Technology leadership | 15 | Does it secure a frontier position (AI, advanced chips, nuclear, bio, quantum)? | First-of-kind in US? Does the frontier move here with it? |
| 4 | Economic impact | 15 | Jobs, anchor effects, regional revitalization | Direct/indirect jobs; supplier ecosystem pulled along; distressed-region siting |
| 5 | Executability | 10 | Will it actually happen on a relevant timeline? | Investor balance sheet real? Site, workforce, power available? Tech proven at scale? |
| 6 | Federal leverage | 10 | Private capital mobilized per federal dollar/risk | Leverage ratio; is federal action necessary or a windfall? |
| 7 | Counterfactual urgency | 5 | What happens without us, and how soon? | Competing-jurisdiction offer live? Investment lost vs. delayed? |

Score each 0–10, multiply by weight/10, sum. **Weights are proposals — the ED should
ratify them, because the weights ARE the strategy.** Revisit annually, not per deal.

## Scoring discipline

- Scored at Screen; re-scored at every stage gate (diligence changes facts).
- Scored by Origination/Analytics, not the deal lead alone — deal teams fall in love.
- Every score of 8+ on dimensions 1–3 requires a cited basis in the deal record
  (import-share data, defense-list reference), not adjectives.
- **Executability is a veto axis:** any deal scoring ≤3 on executability can't be
  Tier 1 regardless of total. Strategic importance doesn't build factories.

## Tiering

| Tier | Threshold | Meaning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | ~75+ or Secretary designation | The deals the office exists for | ED engaged weekly; Secretary visibility; first claim on staff and toolkit |
| **2** | ~55–74 | Important, worth a deal team | Standard process; monthly ED visibility |
| **3** | ~40–54 | Real but not us-scale, or not yet ripe | Light-touch: monitor, or refer to EDA/SelectUSA/state with a warm handoff |
| Declined | <40 | Not strategic or not real | Logged with rationale; referral where useful |

Secretary designation can promote any deal to Tier 1 — the framework informs
judgment, it doesn't replace it. When that happens, record the designation as the
rationale; don't retrofit the scores.

## Portfolio balance view

Rank-ordering isn't enough; the monthly Secretary review shows balance:

- **By sector** — are we concentrated in one sector while a priority sector sits empty?
- **By stage** — is the pipeline front-loaded (all screening, nothing closing) or hollow?
- **By instrument** — are we over-reliant on one agency's authority (concentration
  risk if that program's capacity or appropriations shift)?
- **By timeline** — announcements/closings per quarter; are there dead quarters?

Gaps here feed the origination plan (`origination-analysis.md`) — an empty priority
sector is an origination tasking, not a fact of life.
