# Roles & Hiring Plan

Role cards below define what each seat owns, what "great" looks like, and where the
talent realistically comes from. Hiring waves map to the roadmap phases.

## Leadership spine

### Executive Director
- **Owns:** the mandate; Tier 1 deal outcomes; the Secretary relationship; final
  call within delegated authority.
- **Great looks like:** spends time on the 3–5 deals only the ED can move, not on
  process; delegates everything the decision-rights table allows.

### Chief of Staff
- **Owns:** the buildout itself (this roadmap is their deliverable), operating
  cadence, staffing, budget, and everything without another owner.
- **Great looks like:** the ED never prepares their own briefing book; Phase 0
  exits on time.
- **Source:** existing federal senior staff or returning private-sector operator.

### Head of Deals
- **Owns:** the pipeline end-to-end; assigning deal teams; stage-gate quality;
  deal velocity.
- **Great looks like:** every deal has an owner, a stage, and a next decision date;
  chairs the weekly pipeline meeting.
- **Source:** senior banker / private-equity principal / corp-dev head via IPA or
  appointment.

### Head of Federal Toolkit & Structuring
- **Owns:** mastery of every federal instrument (see `06-toolkit/`); package design
  for every deal; legal, permitting, and incentives sub-teams.
- **Great looks like:** no deal team ever says "we didn't know that authority
  existed"; packages maximize private leverage per federal dollar.
- **Source:** this is the hardest hire — realistically grown from DOE LPO, EXIM,
  DFC, or CHIPS Program Office alumni. Prioritize early.

### Head of Portfolio Management
- **Owns:** everything after close — milestones, monitoring, the government's equity
  and special positions, CHIPS award compliance.
- **Great looks like:** the Secretary gets a quarterly portfolio review with no
  surprises; problems surface early and small.
- **Source:** credit/portfolio background (LPO portfolio side, bank workout groups,
  insurance/pension asset management).

## Deal organization

### Sector Deal Leads (one per priority sector)
- **Owns:** the sector: its deals, its chokepoint map, its target list, its
  industry and agency relationships.
- **Great looks like:** can brief the Secretary on any deal in their sector cold;
  originates at least one top-down deal per year.
- **Source:** mixed — industry operators, bankers with sector books, technical
  experts from national labs or agencies. Sector credibility is non-negotiable.

### Origination & Screening
- **Owns:** the funnel before a deal has a team — intake, screening against the
  scoring framework, channel coverage, the sector heat-map process.
- **Great looks like:** every inbound gets a disposition within two weeks; the
  pipeline is never empty in a priority sector.

## Specialist spine

### Legal & Agreements
- Deal counsel: term sheets, award agreements, equity documentation, delegation
  questions. Source: agency GC offices + private M&A/project-finance lawyers.

### Permitting & Regulatory
- Owns the permitting strategy per deal, the FAST-41 relationship, and state/local
  coordination. Source: Permitting Council, agency NEPA shops, project developers.

### Incentives & Instruments
- The working-level experts of the toolkit function: one person deep on each
  instrument family (credit programs, grants, tax, demand-side). Maintains the
  toolkit playbook as programs change.

### Strategy & Analytics
- Scoring runs, supply-chain analysis, pipeline metrics, briefing production.
  Source: consulting/strategy backgrounds, econ staff.

### Interagency & State Liaison
- Named-counterpart network across agencies and priority states; brokers the
  meetings deal teams need. Source: career interagency veterans.

### External Affairs
- Announcements, congressional interest, industry inbound triage.

## Hiring waves

| Wave | When | Seats | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phase 0–1 | Head of Deals, Head of Toolkit, Permitting lead, CoS (if vacant) | The machine can't run without these four |
| 2 | Phase 1–2 | Sector deal leads to full coverage; 2–3 incentives specialists; analytics | Depth in the sectors where deals concentrate |
| 3 | Phase 2 | Head of Portfolio Mgmt + 2–3 portfolio staff; origination team | The portfolio now exists; stewarding it becomes real work |
| 4 | Phase 3 | Deputies/successors; remaining specialist depth | Durability |

## Talent mechanics (verify each with HR counsel)

- **Detailees** from DOE/DoD/Treasury/EXIM — fastest path, builds the interagency
  network as a side effect; target instrument-owner agencies deliberately.
- **IPA assignments** — for national-lab and university-affiliated technical talent.
- **Special Government Employees** — for part-time senior transaction advisors;
  watch conflict-of-interest screening carefully given the deal flow.
- **Direct/excepted hiring** — whatever authorities Commerce HR can support;
  start the paperwork in Phase 0 because it's the slowest path.

## Two staffing rules

1. **Every deal has exactly one owner.** A deal lead, by name, accountable for the
   whole deal. Specialists support; they never co-own.
2. **Nobody only builds the org.** Everyone except the CoS carries deal work.
   Credibility inside this team comes from deals.
