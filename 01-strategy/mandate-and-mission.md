# Mandate & Mission

## Why this document exists

Everything else — org chart, hiring, process, systems — derives from a precise answer
to "what is this office responsible for, and what may it decide on its own authority?"
This document drafts that answer and lists the questions only the ED (and above) can
resolve. Get this signed off before finalizing the org design.

## Mission (draft)

> The Investment Accelerator delivers the most strategically important private
> investments in the United States: originating them, structuring the federal package
> that makes them happen, clearing the regulatory path, and stewarding the government's
> position after close.

## Mandate scope (from EO 14255 and public practice)

| Function | Description |
|---|---|
| **Facilitation of major investments** | Serve as the front door and deal team for large-scale (roughly $1B+) investments, domestic and foreign-sourced |
| **Federal package assembly** | Identify, negotiate, and stack the federal tools (grants, loans, tax credits, offtakes, equity) that close a deal |
| **Regulatory & permitting navigation** | Reduce regulatory burden on specific deals; coordinate permitting across federal, state, local |
| **Interagency coordination** | Broker agency actions on behalf of deals; single point of accountability |
| **State engagement** | Work with states to leverage their incentives and site readiness |
| **National-lab / research access** | Facilitate investor access to national labs where relevant |
| **CHIPS Program Office** | Administer and renegotiate CHIPS awards; manage the resulting portfolio positions |
| **Portfolio stewardship** | Monitor and manage government interests created by deals (equity stakes, golden-share-type rights, milestone-based awards) |

## What the office is *not* (proposed boundaries)

Boundaries prevent the team from being pulled into everything "investment-shaped":

- Not a general grants administrator — programs stay with their agencies; the
  Accelerator assembles and brokers, it does not run every program it uses.
- Not the owner of trade policy or tariff decisions — it structures deals *around*
  them and feeds deal-relevant intelligence upward.
- Not an economic development office for sub-scale projects — below-threshold
  opportunities get referred to EDA, SelectUSA, or states.
- Not CFIUS — national-security review of foreign acquisitions stays where it is;
  the Accelerator works the promotion side of foreign capital.

Each of these needs explicit ED confirmation because adjacent offices will test them.

## Decision rights (draft — to be ratified)

| Decision | ED | Secretary | White House / interagency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accept a deal into the pipeline | Decide | Informed | — |
| Assign tier / priority | Decide (Tier 2–3) | Approve Tier 1 | Informed on Tier 1 |
| Commit staff to a deal team | Decide | — | — |
| Propose a federal package | Recommend | Approve | Consulted where other agencies' tools are used |
| Terms involving equity or special rights | Recommend | Decide | Consulted / Decide (deal-dependent) |
| Public announcement | Recommend | Approve | Coordinate |
| Kill / deprioritize a deal | Decide (Tier 2–3) | Decide (Tier 1) | Informed |

## Success definition (two-year horizon)

By the end of year two the office should be able to state, with numbers:

1. **Capital facilitated** — total private investment closed with Accelerator involvement
2. **Leverage** — ratio of private capital to federal cost across the portfolio
3. **Speed** — median time from intake to announced deal; permitting time saved
4. **Portfolio health** — % of closed deals on milestone; value of government positions
5. **Coverage** — priority sectors with an active deal or a deliberate "no-play" decision

## Open questions for the ED

1. Where exactly is the threshold for Accelerator involvement — dollar size, sector,
   or Secretary designation — and who grants exceptions?
2. Does portfolio management of CHIPS-derived equity positions sit inside this org
   or in a separate stewardship unit reporting to the Secretary?
3. What hiring authorities are actually available (detailees, IPA, direct hire,
   special government employees), and what is the realistic FTE ceiling?
4. Which decisions is the Secretary willing to delegate to the ED in writing?
   (The table above is a proposal, not a fact.)
5. What is the relationship to NEC/NSC priority-setting — do they task the
   Accelerator, or does the Accelerator propose and they endorse?
6. Is there a legislative strategy to make the office permanent, and does that
   change how we build it?
