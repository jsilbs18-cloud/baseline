## 0. Do this before you sell anything

You bought Intel in **August 2025**. Today is **August 19, 2026**. Your remaining
280 shares are sitting within days of the one-year mark that separates a
short-term gain from a long-term one.

**Pull up the exact lot purchase date in your brokerage right now.**

- Bought **Aug 19–31, 2025** → you must hold until *one year and one day* past
  that date. That could be tomorrow, or three weeks out.

Why it matters: under the kiddie tax (§3) a short-term gain is taxed at your
parents' **ordinary** rate — as much as 37% — while a long-term gain is taxed at
15–20%. On the ~$7,400 gain from a $10,000 sale, selling one day early costs
roughly **$1,500**. On the full 280-share position it's closer to **$4,000**.

This is the single highest-value item in this document and it expires in days.

## 3. The kiddie tax, and why all of this is expensive

The kiddie tax applies to full-time students aged 19–23 whose earned income
doesn't exceed half their support. With your parents covering housing and sending
$2,000/month, that's almost certainly you.

Your unearned income above **$2,700** (2026) is taxed at **your parents' marginal
rate**, not yours. The 0% long-term capital gains bracket that students normally
enjoy does not apply to you. Practically:

| Gain type | Your rate if kiddie tax applies |
|---|---|
| Short-term (held ≤1 yr) | Parents' ordinary rate — up to 37%, plus state |
| Long-term (held >1 yr) | Parents' LTCG rate — 15% or 20%, plus state |

**If you're 24 or older**, none of this applies, you're taxed on your own low
income, and a big chunk of your gains could land in the **0% LTCG bracket**. That
single fact swings several thousand dollars. Tell me your age and I'll redo it.

Not tax advice — confirm with whoever files your return. It touches your parents'
return too, so tell them before you sell anything.

## 6. Cards — the one urgent thing

Chase Total Checking charges **3% on every foreign-currency purchase** plus **$5
per non-Chase ATM withdrawal** abroad, on top of the 3%. On $27,000 of spending
that's roughly **$800 in FX fees** plus a few hundred in ATM fees.

**Open Wise or Revolut today.** Both put a virtual card in Apple Pay within
minutes to a day, both do near-interbank FX, both ship a physical card to your
Paris address. Fund it from Chase now so a balance is waiting when you land.
Demote the Chase debit card to backup.

Worth applying for even though it won't arrive by Friday: **Schwab High Yield
Investor Checking** — no FX fee, reimburses every ATM fee worldwide. Ship it home
and have your parents forward it.

**Never accept "pay in USD"** at a terminal or ATM. That's dynamic currency
conversion — a 3–7% hidden markup on top of everything else. Always choose euros.

## 7. Two things that quietly wreck people

**Losing your US phone number.** You swap your SIM for a French one, your bank
texts a 2FA code to a dead number, and you're locked out of your money in a
foreign country. Before Friday: move your US number to an eSIM and put the French
SIM in the physical slot, or port it to Google Voice, or switch every financial
account to an authenticator app — while you still have a working US line to
receive the setup codes on.

**Booking weekends the week of.** A flight booked Wednesday for Friday runs two
to three times the same seat booked five weeks out. Across 13 trips that gap is
**$1,500–2,500**. Map the weekends in your first two weeks and book the transport
early; hotels can stay flexible on free-cancellation rates.
