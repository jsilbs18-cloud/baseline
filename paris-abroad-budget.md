## 4. The single highest-leverage thing you can do

**Map all 13 weekends in your first two weeks and book the transport early.**

A Friday-morning flight booked Wednesday costs two to three times the same seat
booked five weeks out. Across 13 trips that gap is **$1,500–2,500** — more than
your entire fixed cost for the semester, for an afternoon of planning.

You don't have to lock the whole itinerary. Just book the flights and trains.
Hotels can stay flexible (free-cancellation rates), and where you sleep is the
part you're most likely to change your mind about anyway.

Also worth it in week one:
- **SNCF Carte Avantage Jeune**, €49, 30% off TGV/TER/Intercités, valid to age 27.
  Pays for itself in about two trips.
- **Under-26 free museum entry.** National museums (Louvre, Orsay, Pompidou,
  Versailles) are free for under-26 residents of the EEA. With a validated
  French student visa / titre de séjour you generally qualify. Bring it and try —
  worth several hundred dollars over four months.
- Budget carriers out of Paris: Ryanair, easyJet, Transavia. Note Beauvais is a
  ~75-minute bus from Paris, not really an airport in Paris. Flixbus for cheap
  regional. Eurostar for London.

## 5. Tax — read this before you sell any Intel

You are very likely subject to the **kiddie tax**: it applies to full-time
students aged 19–23 whose earned income doesn't exceed half their support. With
your parents sending $2,000/month, that's almost certainly you.

What it means: your unearned income above **$2,700** (2026) gets taxed at **your
parents' marginal rate**, not yours. The 0% long-term capital gains bracket that
students normally enjoy does not apply to you.

Consequences:

1. **The $10k Intel gain is not $10k.** At a likely 15–20% parental LTCG rate,
   expect roughly **$8,500 net**. Budget the net, set the tax aside now, don't
   discover it in April 2027.
2. **Check the holding period.** If any of those Intel shares are held under a
   year, that's a *short-term* gain — taxed at your parents' **ordinary** rate,
   potentially 32–37%. If a meaningful chunk is close to the one-year mark,
   waiting a few weeks could be worth thousands. Look this up in your brokerage's
   cost-basis view before you sell anything.
3. **This is the real argument against touching the $175k.** A large realization
   at your parents' rates is an expensive way to fund a semester you can already
   afford from cash flow.

None of this is tax advice — confirm with whoever files your return, and note it
affects your parents' return too, so tell them before you sell.

**Timing, since you leave Friday:** if you want the Intel money liquid, start the
sale and the ACH transfer to Chase **this week**. Trades settle T+1 and the
transfer takes another 1–3 business days. Doing this from Paris is doable but
annoying.

## 6. Card and banking setup — do this before Friday

### The problem

Chase Total Checking charges **3% on every foreign-currency purchase**, plus
**$5 per non-Chase ATM withdrawal** abroad — on top of the 3% conversion.

On ~$20,000 of spending that's about **$600 in FX fees**, plus roughly $200 in
ATM fees. **~$800 burned on nothing.**

### The fix, in the two days you have

1. **Open Wise or Revolut today.** Both issue a virtual card into Apple Pay
   within minutes to a day, both do near-interbank FX, both ship a physical card
   to your Paris address. Fund it from Chase by ACH now so there's a balance
   waiting when you land. This is the whole fix, and it's the one thing on this
   page that's genuinely urgent.
2. **Demote the Chase debit card to backup.** Keep it for emergencies. If you
   ever do pull cash from it, pull €300+ at a time so the $5 amortizes.
3. **Amex** stays for emergencies and anything your dad explicitly greenlights.
4. Optional, arrives later: **Schwab High Yield Investor Checking** (no FX fee,
   reimburses every ATM fee worldwide) is the best student-abroad account there
   is. Won't arrive before Friday — apply now, ship to home, have your parents
   forward it.

### Two things that quietly wreck people

- **Never accept "pay in USD"** at a card terminal or ATM. That's dynamic
  currency conversion and it's a 3–7% hidden markup on top of everything else.
  Always choose euros.
- **Do not lose your US phone number.** The classic disaster: you swap your SIM
  for a French one, your bank texts a 2FA code to a dead US number, and you're
  locked out of your money in a foreign country. Before Friday, either move your
  US number to an eSIM (keep the French SIM in the physical slot), or port it to
  Google Voice, or switch every financial account to an authenticator app. Do
  this while you still have a working US line to receive the setup codes on.

## 7. How to actually run it

Don't use a budgeting app — you'll stop opening it by week three. Use accounts
as envelopes so the balance *is* the budget:

| Account | Role | Funding |
|---|---|---|
| **Chase** | Vault. Never spend directly. | Holds everything, including the $3,500 reserve |
| **Wise/Revolut — Living** | Daily Paris life | Top up **$1,800 on the 1st** ($625 fixed + $1,160 discretionary) |
| **Wise/Revolut — Travel** | Trip money only | Top up **$3,250/month**; each trip draws from here |
| **Amex** | Emergencies | — |

If Living runs dry on the 25th, you cook at home for a week. If Travel runs dry,
next weekend is a Paris weekend. That's the whole system — no tracking required,
and the constraint enforces itself.

**Monthly check-in, five minutes on the 1st:** what's left in Chase, are you
above or below the $5,035/month line, and does the trip calendar still look right?
